# Nuclear crest temporal comparison

The prior sample used a narrow symmetric crest with equally shaped shoulders. At early ages its radius was comparable to its width, producing a raised closed wall around a hollow-looking centre. The candidate uses a broader, asymmetric compact crest and a shallower trailing trough, sector-varying strength and a gradual early envelope. Width is 90 times the yield scale; nominal propagation speed, maximum travel and retirement rules are unchanged. Large-yield crest amplitude grows with the fourth root of scale, limiting its increase relative to the prior square-root model. Damage and ordinary wave/tsunami evaluators are untouched.

The profile follows the general established practice of combining controlled, differentiable wave shapes with analytic slopes, rather than implementing a physical tsunami solver. See [NVIDIA GPU Gems: Effective Water Simulation](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models) and the [NOAA long-wave analysis](https://nctr.pmel.noaa.gov/Pdf/tsunami_magnet_Proc-R-Soc-A-20130015.pdf). The specific envelope and shape are artistic approximations, not validated hydrodynamics.

The hidden eight-ship scene triggers one scale-1 wave at frame 100 with a 100 ms fixed simulation step. Frames 120, 150 and 220 show approximately 2, 5 and 12 seconds after launch at 1280×800; camera and source are unchanged. The earlier [5-second image](before/age5.png) shows the closed wall. The candidate frames are [2 s](stronger/frame120.png), [5 s](stronger/age5.png), and [12 s](stronger/frame220.png). These stills show the wall reduced and a broader, less conspicuous swell; the early wave is weak and their spacing cannot establish smooth motion or perceptual acceptance. The scale-1 render was made before the final fourth-root change, which is mathematically identical at scale 1. Higher yields were not rendered in this pass.

On the same hashed libraries and backend sources, CPU wall time around `core_internal.Run()` at 5 s was p50/p95 **8.494/9.401 ms before** and **8.501/9.388 ms after** (50 sampled frames). The complete engine-source fingerprint drifted during the before run; this and the short duration limit attribution. The wider profile scans more area for active waves; GPU cost, long-lived overlap, worst-case load and full game performance remain unmeasured. The user was playing, so no stress run was started.

`geometry-results.txt` records a disposable finite-difference comparison of analytic slopes across scales 1 and 10 and multiple ages: maximum sampled error 0.00083, with bounded heights/foam and a trailing trough when it lies within the sea domain. It does not prove unsampled points or ship response. `before-nuclear.hpp`, `after-nuclear.hpp`, `check.cpp`, and `replay.py` retain inputs; raw frame timings and PPM captures are under `before/` and `stronger/`. The replay uses `FLEET_COMBINED=1`, `WAVE_ABLATE=0`, `WAVE_PICTURE=1`, an existing `WAVE_EVIDENCE_DIR`, `nice -n 15`, and `codex-run-scratch`; it needs the fixture and linked assets documented in the previous material-separation evidence.

The nuclear foam material separation was not edited. A single-wave scene cannot prove that distant tiling or many-wave striping is eliminated in the installed game. No game, save, shared build or installation was operated by the comparison.
