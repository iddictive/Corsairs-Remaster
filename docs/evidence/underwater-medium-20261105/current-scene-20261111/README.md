# Current-source offscreen sea replay

## Frames

In the open-sea near-water frames, sails and clouds occupy the upper area, the water surface and ship silhouettes cross the middle, and the lower area contains the former sky background. The [baseline](baseline-near-2/mode0-depth+0.10-scene.png) shows bright clouds there; the [final candidate](final-matrix/mode0-depth+0.10-scene.png) shows dark blue water with faint residual cloud structure. In [simple sea](final-matrix/mode1-depth+0.10-scene.png), ship reflections remain in the middle band; they were also present in the [baseline](baseline-matrix/mode1-depth+0.10-scene.png).

The [seabed frame](final-seabed/mode0-depth+20.00-scene.png) is filled by a dark teal textured surface with visible seams. The [medium-disabled control](seabed-no-medium/mode0-depth+20.00-scene.png) shows the same full-frame textured surface in brown. The direct `Cuba1_seabed` model is real authored geometry, but this framing does not show an island horizon or prove the normal island lifecycle. The first candidate over-attenuated it; a depth-class visualization returned geometry across the frame and a separate encoded-distance visualization measured approximately 100–120 world units, leading to lower extinction coefficients for geometry. The no-geometry lower sky remains separately attenuated.

## Seabed visibility revision

The next revision keeps the original extinction for empty rays and upward views, but smoothly applies clearer transmission and a bounded soft light lift to finite-depth geometry seen below the horizon. The lift applies to any downward geometry, not a material-identified seabed. It is an approximation rather than a physical lighting calculation.

In the same downward-looking seabed fixture, mean luminance / standard deviation rose from 46.30 / 4.95 to 61.15 / 9.36 on a 0–255 scale in both sea modes. Without the medium, the control measured 53.92 / 9.98. The revised scene is brighter than that control; those statistics establish the measured change, not that the final art direction is accepted.

An additional fixed camera looking toward the seabed horizon shows the water underside across the top, a rocky edge at the horizon, and textured ground below it. Compare [before](geometry-horizon-before/mode0-depth+20.00-scene.png) with [after](geometry-horizon-final/mode0-depth+20.00-scene.png); [mode 1](geometry-horizon-final/mode1-depth+20.00-scene.png) has the same ground and a smoother surface. In the ground region, luminance mean / standard deviation changed from 46.29 / 1.61 to 58.72 / 4.31 in both modes. The first 200 rows, containing the water above, differed in three pixels in mode 0 and zero in mode 1.

A separate depth-mask diagnostic selected about 492,000 empty pixels at camera depth 0.1 and 514,560 at depth 20 in each open-water mode. Against the previous candidate, empty pixels at depth 20 were identical; at depth 0.1, only 19 and 29 pixels differed across the two modes, with signed mean channel differences −0.000139 and +0.000721/255. The small near-surface differences are consistent with scene timing; this comparison is not an exhaustive sky, weather or coastline test.

The 60-frame horizon tests (20 warmup) produced CPU/GPU medians of 8.472/1.492 → 8.535/1.512 ms in mode 0 and 8.337/1.150 → 8.359/1.172 ms in mode 1. These are isolated, sequential samples and do not prove game or battle performance. Previous and final runs used identical 49 library hashes; the old medium source is retained as `geometry-light-before.hpp.txt` and fingerprint-checked by `SCENE_MEDIUM_BASE=1`. The final medium hash is `0e1170728b889e07cc7d724eb6c6fcdda863c88c44ed06d51dc3ca5a0e8dff3b` and native source fingerprint is `bbfab4c5b283504dd45ef7aab5ec62660c1676d1ca5d48f426319d0e3ff396b7`.

## Method and limits

The original `open-sea-replay.py` and `seabed-replay.py` use historical `candidate-sea.cpp`, so they cannot verify the current bridge. `replay.py` here compiles current canonical `backend.mm`, `sea.cpp`, `env_map.cpp`, `core_impl.cpp`, and `sky.cpp` into a private scratch directory, and links copies of the existing prepared libraries. It checks the complete native-source fingerprint and all 49 library hashes before and after the run. The frozen pre-change sea and medium inputs used for the paired baseline are retained here and hash-match the baseline manifest; the later baseline-seabed run explicitly sets `baseline_override`.

The driver uses a hidden window and an isolated fixture and userdata directory. It does not launch the game, read saves, override HOME, or write to the shared build. A fixture-only signal handler passed its SIGABRT guard probe; fixture-only copies of the two core initialization-message sites write to stderr instead of opening dialogs. This does not prove containment of every possible OS-level failure. The scope is an engine-linked diagnostic scene, not the installed user's runtime.

The open-water matrix used 60 frames, a fixed 16 ms simulation step, 1280×800, eight ships, dynamic sky off and no combat. Depth is relative to `WaveXZ` at the camera. Modes 0 and 1 are `SimpleSea=0` and `SimpleSea=1`. The real seabed fixture loads the model directly; its logged camera was approximately 8.15 units below the sampled surface. Each manifest contains source and library fingerprints, and each case retains the frame, raw CPU/renderer traces and log. Above-water baseline/final mean absolute frame differences were 0.084/255 and 0.079/255 respectively; these are not bit-identical frames.

At depth 0.1 in mode 0, baseline → final medians were CPU 8.584 → 8.566 ms and GPU 5.273 → 4.926 ms. The mean absolute RGB difference was 0.053/255 in the upper 180 rows and 123.219/255 in the lower sampled region. In mode 1 the corresponding upper/lower differences were 0.067/123.219. Mode-0 depth 0.5 medians were CPU 8.521 → 8.671 ms and GPU 4.867 → 5.477 ms; the other individual GPU comparisons vary in both directions. These sequential short samples are noisy and do not prove a speedup, unchanged performance, or battle FPS. Raw samples and the full matrix are in `baseline-matrix/` and `final-matrix/`.

An additional [upward-looking near-surface pair](upward-final/mode0-depth+0.10-scene.png) shows clouds across the frame and sail tops at the bottom; compared with the baseline the whole-frame mean absolute difference was 0.014/255. At depth 20 the upward frame shows the underside of the water surface, which alone does not establish sufficient transparency to the sky. A 120-frame paired check (40 warmup) initially gave baseline/candidate GPU medians 5.039/6.101 ms and p95 5.298/6.329 ms. A reverse-order repeat gave candidate/baseline medians 5.020/5.099 ms and p95 5.776/5.324 ms; CPU medians across those four runs were 8.395–8.462 ms. The GPU variation remains unresolved; the samples do not justify a no-regression claim.

`classification/` adds only an isolated `SEA_CLASS` trace to the current sea source. A crest with camera y≈4.60, surface y≈4.80 produced depth≈+0.193 and under=1 in 59 observations. A live whirlpool gave 60 observations in each case: at camera y≈−21.57 and surface≈−21.82 depth≈−0.255 yielded under=0; at y≈−22.07 and surface≈−21.82 depth≈+0.245 yielded under=1. No traced sign/classification mismatch occurred. The selected ordinary-weather trough stayed above world zero, so this does not reproduce a below-zero non-whirlpool trough; the former `y<0` shortcut's potential false positive there is a source-level finding. Trace-enabled runs are not performance evidence.

The empty-sky correction fades over the first 0.12 units and toward the horizon, and does not apply its lower-sky replacement to upward rays or finite-depth geometry. Exactly at the surface the fade is zero; the remaining few centimetres can retain some sky during the transition. The medium still approximates the nearby surface by a plane at the sampled camera height. A continuous camera trajectory, coasts and varying weather, real HUD/audio, and the player's installed gameplay remain unverified.

## Reproduction

From the repository root, use `codex-run-scratch run --max-bytes 2147483648 --max-files 3000 --seconds 240 --reserve-bytes 21474836480 -- python3 docs/evidence/underwater-medium-20261105/current-scene-20261111/replay.py`, with `SCENE_RESULTS` set to a new output directory and `SCENE_CASES='0:-2,0:0.1,0:0.5,0:20,1:-2,1:0.1,1:0.5,1:20'`. `SCENE_BOTTOM=1` selects the authored seabed; add `SCENE_BOTTOM_HORIZON=1` for its horizon view and `SCENE_MEDIUM_BASE=1` for the previous, hash-checked medium. `SCENE_BASELINE=1` selects the earlier hash-checked saved baseline; `SCENE_DISABLE_MEDIUM=1` is the control. `SCENE_LOOK_Y=150 SCENE_FRAMES=120 SCENE_CASES='0:0.1'` selects the upward timing case. Classification uses `SCENE_CLASS_TRACE=1`, `SCENE_CASES='0:-0.2,0:0.2,0:-0.25,0:0.25'`, `SCENE_CASE_SITES='trough,crest,none,none'` and `SCENE_CASE_VORTICES='0,0,1,1'`. These commands read the installed app's resource assets, not the user's saves.
