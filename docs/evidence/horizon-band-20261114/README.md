# Bright sea-horizon band: offscreen investigation

Status: **not fixed or installed**. The player noticed the band in a portfolio test scene and requested an engine repair. The hidden engine-linked fixture loads no save or player input. It reproduces a narrow warm band at the sea horizon under the retained overcast conditions (`overcast.png`), and a more obvious band in a heavier storm. The installed game's appearance is not established by this fixture.

The main sea pass owns the affected pixels: removing SEA from the scene removes the band (`no-sea.png`); removing SKY leaves the band over the sea (`no-sky.png`). Diagnostic omissions of the sky fog dome, the authored-sky lower fill, sea foam, and the sun-road pass did not remove it. A diagnostic Sea2 shader output of the reflection cube had no corresponding warm band. In the depth-refraction path, replacing `skyLocalMedium(...)` with identity transport removes the warm stripe (`no-local.png`), but also removes local atmosphere from water, so it is not an acceptable repair. Applying the full local transport to the main authored sky (`sky-main.png`) visibly discolors the sky and still leaves a horizon discontinuity; this candidate is rejected.

The evidence points to a mismatch at the boundary between local atmospheric transport on distant water and the background. It does not yet prove why that transport differs there, nor an acceptable correction across weather, camera angles, islands and reflection modes. Keep the atmosphere and current renderer behavior until a continuity-preserving fix is proven. The game was running during this investigation; no game interaction, build staging or installation was performed.

## Fixture and limits

`probe.cpp`, `fixture.c` and `engine.ini` record scene setup. Supporting inputs are `docs/evidence/cannon-darts-20261021/native-fixture/{day.c,ship-lights.c,ship-type.c}`, the runtime RESOURCE tree, and the existing native fleet build. The isolated probe was built from the `sea-fleet-benchmark` Ninja compile/link commands using `docs/evidence/particle-tracer-capacity-20261029/build-probe.py.txt`, with the ship include and `STORM_METAL_SHIP_FIRE=1` added. The baseline engine archive was SHA-256 `92cb0ee1a25df5fbba2eda30e3807b0a743e6bbbe57d786ad8af54fab74972ce`; the checkout contained independently changing source, and the original linked probe binary was not retained, so exact reconstruction from a committed revision is not established.

Capture: 96 frames at 16 ms, 2560 × 1600; `STORM_METAL_AA_MODE=3`, `STORM_METAL_MSAA=8`, `STORM_METAL_MODERN_WATER=1`, `STORM_METAL_MODERN_LIGHTING=1`, `STORM_METAL_MODERN_EFFECTS=1`, `STORM_METAL_CINEMATIC=1`, `STORM_METAL_LIGHT_SHAFTS=1`, and `STORM_METAL_SHADOW_QUALITY=2`. `run.log` reports 478 MSAA passes and 96 FXAA frames. The framebuffer was written before the optional frame-trace flush returned false; the fixture consequently exited 75. PNGs were converted from that framebuffer without retouching.

For the `no-local` diagnostic only, the Sea2 modern sky-fog fragment passed `float4(0,0,0,1)` instead of the sampled local volume to `sea2ModernColor`. For `sky-main`, the authored sky was composited with full local transport only on the main backbuffer; this substantially changed the sky and is rejected. No diagnostic source changes were applied to the engine.

At x=200 in the 2560 × 1600 frame, baseline RGB at y=650 was `(180,189,185)` and the identity-transport diagnostic was `(156,186,213)`; above the band at y=640 the baseline was `(157,187,214)`. These pixels establish the local comparison, not whole-scene visual or performance acceptance.
